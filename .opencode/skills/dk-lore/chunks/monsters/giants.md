# The big folk

Strong, patient, slow to anger and slower to forget. Few are cruel; most are only large and
uninterested. They charge **tolls**: the troll under a bridge, the ogre on its track, the orc in
its valley. Travellers pay without argument, or go the long way round.

## Hill folk

- **Orc** (`orc`, 15). A broad, tusked fighter from the hills beyond Narcissa, where orcs keep
	their own laws and feuds. They respect strength more than cleverness and will trade with anyone
	who can lift the same weight. Caravans crossing their valleys pay a toll, then pay another on
	the way back.
- **Uruk** (`uruk`, 20). A tall, disciplined orc soldier in the hills past Narcissa, in squads of
	six or eight. Carries a shield, keeps the line, waits for the captain's orders. Holds a grudge
	against the towns for taking its pasture and says so loudly before a fight. Opens with a war
	cry, eats a herb when wounded. In the open it closes ranks; in a narrow pass it still does.

## The dawk kin

Grey-skinned brutes of the caves, old mines and hill passes past Narcissa. They resist earth; the
Dawk and the Dawkin are weak to fire. Two lines are told of: the miners' gangs (Dawk, Dawkin) and
the pass packs (Dawkor, Dawkare).

- **Dawk** (`dawk`, 19). Broad, grey-skinned, in the caves and old mines past Narcissa. Gangs of
	four or five with clubs taken from the miners they killed. Not clever but patient: one will wait
	a whole day at a cave mouth. Miners leave a lamp burning at the entrance.
- **Dawkin** (`dawkin`, 21). What a Dawk becomes after surviving long enough to learn a few words.
	Leads the gangs in the mines beyond Hambry and keeps the best loot (mostly the miners' gold).
	Retreats when badly hurt and comes back with friends: it drinks a Potion. One that talks has
	usually already decided what to do with you.
- **Dawkor** (`dawkor`, 26). The first of the dawkor kin out of the hills past Narcissa and still
	the smallest: grey skin hard as a sack of gravel, a bone club from an earlier traveller. Bands
	in threes; a lone one is rarely so bold.
- **Dawkare** (`dawkare`, 29). The grown, older kin of the dawkor with a tanner's-leather hide and
	a voice that carries across a valley. They lead the packs and choose their ground, usually a
	narrow pass where a cart must slow: it turns the pass to mud, gives a war cry, steels itself
	under half. Gilead's guard captains tell recruits to watch the rocks above.

## The brute kin

- **Brute** (`brute`, 27). The hired muscle: a head taller than a man and twice as wide. Caravan
	guards out of Gilead say they are not stupid, only uninterested in anything that cannot be
	picked up and thrown. Waits in the pass beyond Narcissa for carts to come up the grade.
- **Large Brute** (`large_brute`, 29). The kin at full size, the one a sensible carter still calls a
	nuisance rather than a disaster. Slow to start (nothing for two turns, then Rage) and very hard
	to stop. Known to tear a loaded wagon apart for the sacks inside.

## Trolls, ogres, giants

Each smashes a standing stone that leans over you, or fells a dead tree onto you, once a round
has gone by (`things/battlefield-objects.md`).

- **Troll** (`troll`, 17). Sleeps under bridges and along the old road between Midworld and Roma;
	most travellers pay the toll. Strong, stupid in the way of hungry things, slow to forget a
	grudge. Regenerates under 60 percent life, unless it is burning or in sun or heat; weak to
	fire. A troll caught in daylight is said to turn to stone, "though the stone ones are usually
	the ones that were already dead".
- **Ogre** (`ogre`, 23). A huge, dull-witted giant in the hills east of Gilead that eats whatever
	comes up the track. Keeps a cave and a hut of stolen timber, and collects bones and broken
	carts as trophies. Slow to anger; once angry it chases a fleeing man a mile. Traders pay the
	toll or go the long way round. Hunters near Hambry trade Ogre-hide Cuirasses as trophies
	(`things/gear.md`).
- **Giant** (`giant`, 43). Keeps to the high valleys beyond Gilead where the snow lasts into
	summer. Carries a whole ox up a cliff for the pleasure of eating it at the top. They do not
	hate men so much as forget them, which is worse. Hunters go in pairs and leave a little salt
	meat on a rock for its goodwill. Hurls boulders every third turn, each costing it a tenth of its
	life.

## Of the upper echelon

- **Titan** (`titan`, 50). A giant of living rock who sleeps in the mountains above Endworld; the
	peaks shake when he turns over. The oldest of the great beings the road-wardens know, he
	remembers the first towns being built. He does not hate men: he does not see them until they
	stand on his foot. Every 4th turn the mountain draws back for a stomp (every 3rd under half
	life), with boulders in between, and under a third he digs in. Immune to poison; resists earth;
	weak to lightning and wind.
- **Colossus** (`colossus`, 49). A statue of stone and iron raised in the old wars to guard the road
	beyond Endworld. Its masters died, and it has stood ever since obeying the last order it was
	given: slow, hits very hard, and does not forgive anyone who crosses the line painted at its
	feet. Wards off sleep, poison, venom and burning.
