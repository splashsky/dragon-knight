# Sayings, rumours and habits

Short lines the people of the world repeat. Each is attributed to the trade or town that says it; the quotes are
the lore's own.

## Sayings

**About ground and weather**

- "A wet road is worse than a long one." (the carters of Bris)
- "A morning fog is the best time of the year for business." (the bandits of Narcissa)
- The heat is what Endworld breathes out. (the people of Roma)
- "A wet man is hard to burn and easy to shock." (fishermen of the Shoals)
- The sand is an enemy that never tires, and it wins the long fight. (nomads of the dry country)
- "The bog takes what it likes, and it likes whatever is slow." (the folk of the Thornbog)
- "A fight on a ledge goes to whoever keeps his feet." (herders of the passes beyond Kalle)
- "The man on the higher side has the better of it." (shepherds of the downs)
- "The forest is kind to anyone who knows where the roots lie." (hunters of the Weald)
- "A diver who is afraid of the water is the first to drown." (pearl divers of Bris)
- A man who fears fire should stay out of the ash, and the ash will find him anyway. (Endworld)
- "A fall on ice is the one thing a Warrior cannot argue his way out of." (old hands in Hambry)
- "A cactus has more sense than a well." (nomads)

**About fighting**

- "The sun rises for the one who is still standing." (Paladins of Roma)
- "A wounded man should not wait for the dawn to come to him." (priests of Bris)
- "A man who will not step back never needs healing in time." (Warriors in Kalle)
- "Cast it first and talk later." (the guild masters of Roma)
- "A fighter who makes the ground his own does not have to be the strongest man on it." (the yard sergeants)
- "A swing drawn back for a long time is the one to get out of the way of." (carters)
- "A standard that falls takes the courage with it." (the ballads of the Barrens)
- "A sleeping man out there is a dead one." (the wardens of Endworld)
- "Bris has a saying about men who fight angry and lose their heads."

**About magic and learning**

- "The Mage who lives on other people's blood is still a Mage, but a thin one." (old Gilead)
- "A clear eye is worth more than a clever one." (the monk above Hambry)
- A dull fight pays the pit master less. (the pit master of Kalle)
- "A Mage who can throw wind is never short of a way to move the furniture." (the masters of Midworld)
- "A warm hand is a lucky one." (the Bris sellers of rings)

**About the small things**

- "Nobody in Midworld has ever died of a single dose, only of three."
- "A shadow only follows people who have something to hide." (gravediggers)
- "A raven that circles overhead is waiting for someone to fall." (hunters)
- "Slimes are what is left when a spell goes wrong."
- A pinch of salt sends a red slime down the drain, though it never works twice. (cellar keepers)

## Motifs that recur

- **Debts and ledgers.** The dead who stay owe or are owed: a Ghost "moaning about a debt nobody remembers",
	a Spirit "who died owing a debt", a Dybbuk who takes someone "with a debt left unpaid to its family", a
	Specter "counting his debts", a Phantom "who died without his pay". The Greater Devil has "a ledger that never
	closes", the Grey Daemon "made a bargain with the wrong ledger", the Liche Lord is "very fond of his records",
	and the Advocate asks for a receipt.
- **Counting.** The Megadoomer counts, the Ultradoomer's count "is nearly done", the Demon Prince will "count the
	dead", the Liche King "counts every coin himself", the Doom is a count with a date on it.
- **Do not answer.** Do not answer when a spirit calls your name, nor when Lucifuge's name is called, nor
	the Megadoomer's count ("those who answer its count find the number running down a little faster in their
	own heads").
- **Something shiny.** A raven steals buckles and a Black Dragon "leaves a traveller alone if he leaves it
	something shiny". Gamblers carry a fortune coin, and the thieves who carried them "never went hungry".
- **Tolls.** Trolls, ogres, orcs, the Renegade and the Liche King's collectors all ask for one.
- **Bells.** Bells on Narcissa's walls, on Hambry's gate, a bell buoy in the shoals and the silent bell of the
	old chapel on the downs: bells tell the lost where the road is, or that it is no longer there.
- **Silver and honest smiths.** Silver ends werewolves and lycans, "which is why the smiths charge double for it",
	and the smiths "are not always honest about its purity".
- **The price comes later.** Demon's Lie "promises fortune and delivers a broken body"; Demon's Sin has a price
	"the body pays"; Fury's rage stays after the blood has gone; the Glass Blade is sold "with a warning on the box".

## How the lore talks

- A trade says a thing and the next clause takes it back: priests in Endworld claim the Dragon's Tear steadies the
	grip, "which mostly means they sell it at a high price."
- "Nobody has ever..." and "Nobody has yet found..." lead to a dry fact: "Nobody has yet found a way to make one
	purr."
- A joke is told by the person who paid for it: "Wise travellers look like cooks when passing through."
- Where the lore is unsure, sages say and scholars argue. Where it is sure, it gives the habit.
